# A Note for School Leaders

**About "Using AI at Home and School"**

## What this is

This is a one-page, printable standard. It states, for three age bands (8-11, 12-15, 16-18), what AI use is fine, what an adult should check, and what any AI tool must refuse to do. A principal can sign it. A parent can put it on the fridge. It is written to be read in under a minute per age band, in plain language, with no jargon and no citations on the page itself.

## What this is not

This is not a product, and it is not for sale. There is no account to create, no software to install, no subscription, and no vendor relationship attached to reading or adopting it. It is offered publicly and free of charge, for any school or family to use, print, or adapt as they see fit. Nobody profits if your school adopts it, and nobody loses anything if you don't.

## Why it exists

Right now, no shared answer exists for what a nine-year-old should be allowed to do with AI, or a fifteen-year-old. Every parent and every teacher improvises alone. Meanwhile the sharp end of the risk is documented and real: chatbots engaging children on self-harm, AI-generated sexual imagery of real kids, and companion apps built to feel like a friend rather than disclose that they are software. This standard puts a plain-language floor under all of that, one page, for a home or a school to point to.

## Exactly what I'm asking of you

Ten honest minutes. Read the standard (attached). Then tell me, from what you actually see in your building, where it's wrong. A few concrete questions that would help most:

- Is there a rule in your column that doesn't match a real situation your staff has run into?
- Is anything missing that you'd want a tool to refuse, and it currently doesn't?
- Is anything on the page you would never actually be able to enforce?

That's the whole ask. I'm not looking for an endorsement, a signature, or a commitment today, just your honest read as someone who deals with this in real classrooms, not in the abstract.

## How to respond

Reply directly to the email this was attached to, whichever way is easiest for you: a few sentences, a marked-up copy, or a five-minute call. All of it helps.

## A note on where this stands

The standard is version 1.2, reviewed most recently in August 2026, and is intended to be revisited annually as the landscape changes. It reflects publicly reported incidents and emerging state-level rules on AI and children, folded in through several rounds of review. It is a living document, not a one-off flyer, and feedback from people actually running schools is the main way it gets better.
